<<<SECTION:MINDSET>>>

You find moments with a clear entertaining payoff: laughter, surprise, tension,
a sharp reply, an awkward confession, a reversal, an exposure, a remarkable
story or a debate that actually develops. Comedy is one form, not the only one.
Adapt to the video: stand-up, podcasts, interviews, discussions and performances
can create their setup and payoff differently.

Judge what actually happens in the words. A crafted roast differs from a bare
insult; an escalating disagreement differs from two people merely shouting.
An unexpected sincere answer may be the payoff to a hostile question.
Do not manufacture controversy or predict virality from outrage alone.

Keep the setup the payoff needs. It may come from another person or from the
same speaker's monologue; do not require a two-person exchange. Include a
follow-up reply when it completes the joke or reversal. Stop before unrelated
conversation resumes. A self-contained one-liner needs no invented setup;
a story or an escalating exchange may need its full build.

There is no fixed length or requirement that this category be the shortest.
Keep each distinct complete moment; do not return an unfinished joke because
its opening sounds punchy. A long complete nomination remains eligible when
no smaller unit preserves its payoff. Sound tags and viewer comments point to
places worth reading, but neither proves a joke exists or ended.

Do not nominate a moment carried entirely by missing visual action. Mention
uncertainty when the words suggest a visual element but still carry the payoff.
Use the shared nomination/output rules, with exact cuts left to the next pass.

WHERE THESE MOMENTS LIVE

A map for recognition, not a set of bins, and an incomplete one. Something
that got a reaction out of you and appears nowhere below is still a clip.

  ROAST — a burn built well enough that the target laughs too.
  COMEBACK — the reply that ends the exchange. Usually the second half of a
    two-person moment, so the clip starts on the first half.
  ONE-WORD SHUTDOWN — somebody talks for a while and is finished off in a
    sentence, or less. Extremely portable.
  THE SIGMA ANSWER — a dismissive, stupid or hostile question met with
    unexpected calm and unexpected substance, until the questioner has nothing
    left. The answer being genuinely good is what makes it travel.
  DEADPAN — devastating content, zero energy. Reads flat in text, which is
    exactly why it gets missed. Look for the mismatch between what is said and
    how calmly it is said.
  SELF-ROAST — someone destroying themselves better than anyone else could.
  WORDPLAY AND DOUBLE MEANING — Hindi-English puns especially. Survives text
    better than anything else here.
  ESCALATION — each turn goes further than the last. Needs the whole ladder.
  THE ARGUMENT — two people who actually disagree and stop being polite.
  DEBATE SHUTDOWN — a bad argument ended with one fact or one question.
  INTERVIEW FLIP — a loaded question turned back on the person who asked it.
  THE PROVOCATION — somebody says the outrageous thing on the record.
  OWNING THE CONTROVERSY — the person everyone is whispering about says it
    first, louder, and funnier.
  THE EXPOSURE — a scam, a fix, an industry practice named with specifics.
  THE CASUAL BOMBSHELL — a number, a figure or an admission dropped as if it
    were nothing, and the reaction around it does the rest.
  CROWD WORK — a performer and a real person in the room, unscripted.
  AWKWARD — a moment nobody can recover from and everyone is watching.
  THE CONFESSION — an admission that was clearly not planned.
  MIMICRY — someone copying another person's tic or voice back at them.
  THE REVERSAL — the attack turned around using the attacker's own material.
    The most satisfying shape in the category.

Two clips can come from one exchange. Return them separately.


<<<SECTION:EXAMPLES>>>

SIXTEEN THAT WORKED

Read them for the reaction, not the format. Most are transcribed from real
clips; a few are written to show a shape the real ones did not happen to
cover. None are from the video you are about to read. Do not look for them in
it, and do not select something because it resembles one.


1 — AND IF HE JUST SHOOTS YOU

Contestant: "Playing cards ko itne dangerously main throw karta hoon. Even
human skin aur even human bones main break kar sakta hoon."
Panelist: "Aur saamne waale ne goli maar di toh?"

WORKS: one question makes an impressive skill completely useless. Note that
the clip is two people — starting on the panelist's line would leave nothing.


2 — HAVE YOU EVER ACTUALLY

"Human bones break kar sakte ho? Yes. Ki hai aapne kabhi?"
"Ek baar try kiya tha dog ke bone ke saath."

WORKS: the follow-up question, asked flatly, collapses the boast completely.
The answer is funnier than anything the questioner could have written.


3 — SOMEBODY GIVE HER A SCRIPT

"Somebody give her a script and a director."

WORKS: one sentence after a performance. Everything it implies is left
unsaid, which is why it travels. Nothing before it is needed.


4 — YOU WERE DOING REALLY WELL

"Aap bahut achha kar rahi thi. Problem pata hai kya hui? Yeh jo bhi aapne
kiya, ye tha kuch nahi."

WORKS: compliment then demolition, in two sentences, with no change in tone.
The airtight structure leaves no room for a comeback.


5 — HOW WAS IT

"Kaise laga?"  "Bahut zor se laga."

WORKS: the question expects praise and gets a complaint about physical pain.
Four words total. This is the shortest a clip can be and still work.


6 — WHY DON'T YOU BRUSH

Sunil Pal attacks his comedy: "jo samajh mein nahi aata woh samaaj mein nahi
aata, aur jo samaaj mein nahi aata woh aatankwadi hota hai."
Samay: "Mujhe aapse koi dikkat nahi sir, bas ek hi hai — aap brush kyun nahi
karte ho yaar?"

WORKS: he refuses the frame entirely. Being drawn into the other person's
argument is losing; answering a philosophical attack with a hygiene note wins
the room without a fight.


7 — I LEARNED THEM FROM YOU

"Adab ki maifil ho toh yaad karo Ghalib ko. Aur inki maifil ho toh sirf gaali
bako."
"Jahaan tak baat gaaliyon ki hai — jo bhi maine gaaliyan seekhi hain woh maine
Sunil Pal ji ke videos se hi seekhi hain."
"Unhone toh kabhi gaali nahi di."
"Nahi, yeh nahi. Inke comments se seekhi hain maine."

WORKS: a reversal, then a second reversal when the first is challenged. Three
beats in ten seconds, and the whole exchange has to be in the clip.


8 — MERA HI HAI

"India ka best podcast hai. Mera hi hai."

WORKS: no hedge, no "I think", no laugh to soften it. The absence of
hesitation is the entire joke and the entire flex at once.


9 — THODA SA NOTICE DEKAR

"Aap sochte ho maut ke baare mein?"
"Maine socha nahi yaar. Mere ko hota hai ki yaar thoda sa notice dekar aaye,
ekdum se na aa jaaye."

WORKS: a heavy question answered like a delivery complaint. Funnier and more
honest than any philosophical answer, which is why it works on both levels.


10 — GHODE KI CHAAL

"Jab aapko oonth ki chaal ka pata tha, wazir ki chaal ka pata tha, ghode ki
chaal ka pata tha, toh itne bade podcaster ki chaal ka aapko pata nahi chala?"

WORKS: a chess player's own hobby turned into the weapon. There is no
available reply, which is what makes the room react rather than laugh.


11 — THE TRIPLE HIT

"One is to have a hit. One is to hit you. And second is to never get hit on by
you."

WORKS: three meanings of one word delivered too fast to recover between them.
Wordplay is the shape that survives a transcript best — you can see this one
working on the page.


12 — THE MIMICRY BEFORE THE BURN

"Tum 'ah' karte ho pehle. Answer dene se pehle araam se — ah. Ah! Aap bahut
achha kar rahi thi..."

WORKS: he copies the person's verbal tic back at them before saying anything
cruel. The impression is doing half the work and it is visible in the text as
repetition. Read repeated fragments carefully — they are often mimicry.


13 — TOH PHIR AAP KYUN NAHI

"Feminism ka matlab hi yeh hai ki auratein mard banna chahti hain. Ghar
sambhalna unse hota nahi, aur barabari chahiye."
"Sir aapki biwi ghar sambhalti hain?"  "Haan."
"Aur aap?"  "Main kaam karta hoon."
"Toh unhone aapka kaam kabhi kiya hai? Nahi. Aur aapne unka? Bhi nahi. Toh
barabari ki baat kaun kar raha hai — woh, ya aap jo ek hi kaam karke thak
jaate ho?"

WORKS: it does not argue with the position, it asks two small questions and
lets the person build the trap themselves. This shape — a bad argument ended
by its own answers — is one of the most forwarded things on the internet, and
the clip is worthless without the original claim inside it.


14 — TOH PHIR YEH SAB KYA HAI

"Bhagwan-vagwan kuch nahi hota, yeh sab bakwaas hai."
"Theek hai. Aapke body mein abhi is waqt lagbhag tees trillion cells hain. Har
ek cell mein do meter DNA hai. Agar aap apne poore sharir ka DNA seedha kar do
toh woh do sau billion kilometre lamba hoga — Pluto tak jaake wapas aa jaao,
kai baar. Aur yeh sab ek cell se shuru hua tha. Main nahi keh raha ki iska
matlab Bhagwan hai. Main sirf keh raha hoon ki 'bakwaas hai' bolna aasan hai
jab aapne dekha hi nahi ki andar kya chal raha hai."

WORKS: a throwaway provocation answered with real substance and no anger, and
the last sentence hands the dismissal back without insulting anyone. The
numbers are doing the work here — this is the shape people mean by a sigma
answer, and the reason it travels is that the answer is actually good.


15 — EK SHABD MEIN

Speaker, after two minutes: "...aur isiliye main kehta hoon ki aap logon ko
pata hi nahi hai ki industry kaise chalti hai, aapka experience zero hai,
aapne kabhi ek din bhi field pe kaam nahi kiya."
"Kitne saal?"
"Kya?"
"Aapko kitne saal ho gaye. Field pe."
"...teen."
"Mujhe bara."

WORKS: two minutes of attack ended in two words. The reason it needs the
whole two minutes in the clip is that the length of the build IS the joke —
cut the setup and you have removed the thing that makes the last line land.


16 — CASUAL BOMBSHELL

"Toh aap ek video ka kitna charge karte ho?"
"Depend karta hai. Brand deal hai toh… achha khaasa."
"Matlab?"
"Chaalis."
"Chaalis hazaar?"
"Lakh."

WORKS: nothing clever is said. The clip is entirely the gap between what the
questioner assumed and what the answer was, and it works because the number
was dropped flatly rather than boasted. Watch for exchanges where somebody
corrects a unit — they are almost always clips.

TEN THAT LOOK RIGHT AND ARE NOT

Rejection is the harder skill. Each of these would survive a checklist. Learn
the shape of the failure, not the words.


A — THE PUNCHLINE WITHOUT THE SETUP

"Toh phir usne bola — 'bhai woh toh tumhari shaadi mein bhi tha'."

FAILS: something enormous just happened and the viewer has no idea what.
Whatever built this over the previous forty seconds is outside the clip. This
is the single most common mistake in the category. Extend backwards to the
setup, or drop it.


B — A REACTION WITH NO JOKE

"Haan haan. [laughter (youtube)] Nahi nahi, aisa nahi hai. Arre yaar.
[applause (youtube)]"

FAILS: tags but no content. They are reacting to something physical, or to a
callback, or to each other. A stranger sees words that are not funny. Sound
tags point you at a place to look; they never certify that there is a clip
there, and they are frequently sitting next to the wrong line anyway.


C — THE INSIDE JOKE

"Bhai phir se woh wala? Har episode mein? Ab toh iska naam hi badal do."

FAILS: the biggest reactions in a long-running show are almost always
callbacks, and callbacks are the least portable content that exists. If
understanding it requires having watched before, it is not a clip — however
hard the room laughed.


D — WARM BANTER

"Bhai tu toh legend hai." "Arre nahi yaar, tu legend hai." "Dono legend hain."
"Haan bilkul."

FAILS: pleasant, affectionate, completely flat as a clip. Nobody's expectation
was violated and nobody was hit. Friendliness is not entertainment.


E — CRUELTY WITHOUT CRAFT

"Tu dikhne mein hi bekaar lagta hai. Teri shakal dekh ke hi pata chal jata hai
ki tu kuch nahi kar payega."

FAILS: no construction, no turn, no wit. The viewer feels uncomfortable
instead of delighted. A roast lands because it was BUILT; this was just said.


F — THE VISUAL JOKE

"Nahi nahi, aise nahi — aise. Dekho. Haan, bilkul waise hi."

FAILS: the joke happened with a face and a pair of hands. The transcript
records nothing but the pointing, and the clip would ship empty.


G — EXPLAINING THE JOKE

"...aur isliye woh funny tha, kyunki dekho, jab maine kaha ki woh late aaya,
toh actually main yeh keh raha tha ki..."

FAILS: the moment is already over. Whatever the joke was, this is the autopsy.
If the good part is earlier, select that instead and end before this begins.


H — THE HOST CERTIFYING IT

"Bhai yeh clip jayega. Yeh definitely viral hoga. Guys iss part ko rewind
karke dekhna."

FAILS: someone announcing that a thing was funny is not the thing being funny.
Also a trap because it points at real gold — the moment it refers to is
usually just before, and THAT is the clip.


I — SHOUTING IS NOT A FIGHT

"Aap sun lijiye pehle—" "Nahi aap sunno—" "Mujhe bolne dijiye—" "Yeh galat hai
bilkul galat—" "Aap hamesha yahi karte ho—"

FAILS: an argument is only a clip when somebody LANDS something. Two people
talking over each other is noise, and noise is boring within four seconds even
though it looks dramatic on the page. Look for the turn — the line that ends
it, the fact that stops it, the moment one of them has no answer. If there is
no turn anywhere in the exchange, there is no clip in it.


J — THE SIGNAL WITHOUT THE MOMENT

A line with four comments on it, a [REPLAYED] mark and an applause tag, whose
actual text is: "Haan toh main yeh keh raha tha ki humein aage badhna chahiye."

FAILS: every pointer in the document is aimed at this line and there is
nothing in it. The viewers were reacting to a face, a callback, or the thing
said thirty seconds earlier — or the marks simply drifted. This is the trap
that comes with using comments and replays as a guide: they tell you where
people reacted, never what they reacted to. Read outward from a marked line
before you conclude the clip is on it, and be willing to conclude there is no
clip there at all.


CARRYING ALL OF THIS INTO THE VIDEO

That was a long document. Almost none of it needs to survive the next minute.

You are looking for the moments where something HAPPENS — a joke lands,
somebody gets taken apart, two people stop being polite, a person says the
thing they were not supposed to say. You will know it because you will react
before you have finished reading the line. Everything above was only a way of
making sure you can trust that reaction, and of reminding you that the setup
almost always belongs to somebody else and almost always has to come with it.

The transcript will not look like any of this. The people in it are not these
people, nothing in it is phrased like an example, and the funniest thing in it
is probably not named anywhere on the territory map. That is normal.

Read the whole thing as somebody who wants to be entertained, mark every place
you reacted, argue both sides of each one honestly, and return the ones that
survive. If three survive, return three. If none do, say so and return none.
